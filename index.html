Intramuscular (IM)
  • Angle: 90°
  • Gauge: 20 - 25G
  • Needle: 1 to 1.5 inches
  • Sites: Deltoid (≤ 1 mL), Ventrogluteal, Vastus Lateralis (peds).
Subcutaneous (SC)
  • Angle: 45° or 90° (if thick fold)
  • Gauge: 25 - 31G
  • Needle: 3/8 to 5/8 inch
  • Sites: Abdomen, outer upper arm, anterior thigh.
Intradermal (ID)
  • Angle: 10° - 15°
  • Gauge: 25 - 27G
  • Needle: 1/2 inch
  • Sites: Inner forearm, upper back. Creates wheal/bleb.
Toxin / Medication Antidote / Reversal Agent Key Nursing Action
Acetaminophen (Paracetamol)N-Acetylcysteine (Mucomyst)Most effective within 8 hrs of ingestion.
Opioids (Morphine, Fentanyl)Naloxone (Narcan)Monitor RR; short half-life may require repeated doses.
HeparinProtamine SulfateAdminister slowly IV (1 mg per 100 units heparin).
Warfarin (Coumadin)Vitamin K (Phytonadione) / FFPMonitor INR value before administration.
Benzodiazepines (Ativan, Valium)Flumazenil (Romazicon)Caution: May induce seizures in chronic users.
DigoxinDigoxin Immune Fab (Digibind)Monitor for hypokalemia & severe dysrhythmias.
MONA (Chest Pain / MI)
  • M: Morphine (for unrelieved pain & anxiety)
  • O: Oxygen (if SpO₂ < 90%)
  • N: Nitroglycerin (vasodilation)
  • A: Aspirin (162-325 mg chewed)
SAMPLE (Health History)
  • S: Signs & Symptoms
  • A: Allergies
  • M: Medications
  • P: Past Medical History
  • L: Last Oral Intake
  • E: Events Leading to Illness
PQRST (Pain Assessment)
  • P: Provoking / Palliating factors
  • Q: Quality of pain (sharp, dull, throbbing)
  • R: Region & Radiation
  • S: Severity (0 to 10 scale)
  • T: Timing (onset, duration, frequency)
FAST (Stroke Assessment)
  • F: Facial Droop
  • A: Arm Drift
  • S: Speech Difficulty
  • T: Time to call emergency response
